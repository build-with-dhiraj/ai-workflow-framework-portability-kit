# Cover Letter

The resume/CV must stand alone — many readers never open the CL. The CL **deepens**, it does not duplicate. Every major CL claim traces to a resume bullet; it adds motivation, "why this institution," and (academic) research vision.

## Detect institution type first
- **Industry** — any company. HR likely reads first → minimize jargon.
- **National Lab** — DOE/government research facility. Domain expert reads → technical vocabulary OK.
- **Academic** — university postdoc/faculty. Domain expert reads.

## Structure by type

**Industry — 250–300 words, 3 paragraphs**
- P1 Hook: connect their product/technology to your achievement; state identity + position; specific opener, not generic.
- P2 Evidence: 2–3 achievements as business value; ≤3–4 quantified claims; mirror JD terms.
- P3 Close: forward-looking value + active call to action. If pivoting, frame what industry *enables* (not what academia lacked).

**National Lab — 350–450 words, 4 paragraphs**
- P1 mission alignment + division/group + position (cite a programmatic thrust or group publication).
- P2 current work with mission framing; theory↔experiment bridge; HPC scale; collaborative tone.
- P3 transferable methodology arc; quantify.
- P4 programmatic vision + collaboration offer + seminar availability ("thrust area," "programmatic direction").

**Academic — 350–450 postdoc / 450–650 faculty, 4 paragraphs**
- P1 connect to the PI's specific paper; name the PI; your identity + position.
- P2 current research with field context; **future direction 1–2 sentences mandatory**.
- P3 prior foundation + collaboration + mentorship; faculty: departmental-fit narrative.
- P4 close forward-looking; name 2–3 faculty for collaboration.

## Universal rules
Specific opener (never "I am writing to express my interest"). Add context the resume can't. ≤3–5 quantified claims. Credentials woven into body, not dumped in closing. Active close (not "Thank you for your consideration"). If pivoting, lead with methodology in P1, never apologetic ("Despite my background…"). **Max 2 em-dashes** for the whole letter. Then run the [ai-fingerprint.md](ai-fingerprint.md) 12-item scan — CLs are the most vulnerable document.

## Hook verification (MANDATORY before showing a draft)
Web-search and verify every external reference:
- Academic: PI name + cited paper title/journal/year.
- Lab: named program / thrust area / group publication.
- Industry: product name / technology claim / company news.

Verified → note the source URL in the session file. Unverified → label it **"UNVERIFIED — please confirm"** in the draft. Never guess names, titles, or journal details.

## Critique checklists (used by [critique.md](critique.md) §7)
- **Anti-patterns:** no generic opener; no CV rehash in prose; names a specific PI/group/product/paper; clear "why THIS place"; strongest qual in P1; no defensive language; active close; credentials in body not closing.
- **Tailoring:** names specific PI/group/program or product/tech; ≥3 JD terms that supplement (not duplicate) resume keywords; references mission/culture/recent work; proposes a specific method↔need connection; correct institution type & tone.
- **Context-specific:** Industry — business-value translation, "why industry" positive, jargon minimized. Lab — mission alignment in P1, HPC/collaboration, lab vocabulary. Academic — PI named with research connection, future direction present, departmental fit.
- **Structural:** consistency with resume; complementarity (adds what resume can't); word count in range; tone match; 3–5 quantified claims; methodology-led if pivoting.
- **Package cohesion:** resume stands alone; CL deepens not introduces; no contradictions (dates/metrics/framing); complement not repeat; page budget (resume+CL = 3pp, CV+CL = 6–7pp).
