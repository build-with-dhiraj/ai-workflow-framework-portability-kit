# Research & Domain-Specialist Lens

Two outputs: (1) a **Success Profile** (what the role needs) and (2) a **Domain-Specialist Lens** (who reads the resume and what makes them say yes). Persist both to the session file — the critique reuses the lens without re-researching.

## 1. Parse the JD

```
Extract from this JD:
1. EXPLICIT REQUIREMENTS — must-have vs nice-to-have
2. TECHNICAL KEYWORDS & domain terminology (verbatim)
3. IMPLICIT PREFERENCES — cultural signals, hidden requirements
4. RED FLAGS — over/under-qualification risks, mismatches
5. ROLE ARCHETYPE — IC technical / people leadership / cross-functional; academic vs industry
```
Read the JD **three times**: first for requirements, second for culture signals, third for vocabulary.

## 2. Research the company (WebSearch)

```
"{company} mission values culture"
"{company} engineering blog" / "{company} research"
"{company} recent news product launches"
"{company} team structure {department}"
```
Synthesize: mission & values, cultural priorities, business/revenue model, R&D culture (academic-leaning / patent-driven / product-shipping / mission-driven), company stage, and the vocabulary that signals "insider."

## 3. Benchmark the role (optional, WebSearch + WebFetch)

```
site:linkedin.com {job_title} {company}      → fetch top 3–5 profiles
fallback: site:linkedin.com {job_title} {similar_company}
```
Extract: common backgrounds/paths, emphasized skills, the terminology they use, recurring accomplishment themes.

## 4. Build the Domain-Specialist Lens (7 elements)

Generated fresh per JD — no static templates. Flag any assumptions.

1. **Reviewer persona** — who actually reads this? Title, seniority, daily work, how many CVs they've seen for this posting, what makes them roll their eyes, what would genuinely impress them.
2. **Company context** — what they make/sell/research; R&D culture; strategic bets; insider vs outsider vocabulary.
3. **JD vocabulary extraction** — the 8–10 most important terms ranked by frequency, placement (title/header vs body), and binary-capability vs spectrum-skill. For each: what does *this* company mean by it?
4. **Domain vocabulary map** — 5–8 swaps that separate "outsider applying" from "insider who gets it":

   | Resume currently says | Should say for THIS JD | Why |
   |---|---|---|
5. **Gap ranking** — **Fatal** (binary capabilities the JD requires, terms in the title, phrases repeated 3+×) / **Serious** (preferred quals competitive candidates will have) / **Cosmetic** (buried nice-to-haves most also lack). For each: bridgeable truthfully, or hard limitation?
6. **Methodology transfer test** — for each of the candidate's top 5 achievements, write one sentence on how an expert at *this* company sees it mapping to *their* work. If you can write it naturally → bridged. If you struggle → not explicit enough. If you can't honestly → hard gap, not a reframing problem.
7. **Competitive landscape** — who's the "obvious fit" candidate, what do they have that this candidate doesn't, and what does this candidate uniquely offer? This sets what to EMPHASIZE vs BRIDGE.

## 5. Success Profile (synthesis)

```
## Success Profile: {Role} at {Company}
### Core Requirements (must-have) — {req}: {evidence}
### Valued Capabilities (nice-to-have) — {cap}: {why it matters here}
### Cultural Fit Signals — {value}: {how to demonstrate}
### Narrative Themes — {theme}: {examples from similar role holders}
### Terminology Map — standard term → company-preferred term
### Gap Assessment — Direct / Bridge (with confidence) / Gap (cannot claim)
### Risk Factors — {concern}: {mitigation}
```

The **Gap Assessment** feeds [discovery.md](discovery.md). The **lens** feeds [matching.md](matching.md) and [critique.md](critique.md).
